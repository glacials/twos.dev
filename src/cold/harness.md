# Harnesses Are Just Brain Tricks

The chronology of agent harnessing shares a lot with the chronology of
human knowledge tooling.


<table class="agent-comparison">
  <thead>
    <tr>
      <th>Problem</th>
      <th>Agent solution</th>
      <th>Human solution</th>
    </tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr>
      <td data-label="Problem">Agents cannot effectively review their own work</td>
      <td data-label="Agent solution">Get another agent to do the review</td>
      <td data-label="Human solution">Peer review</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td data-label="Problem">
        Context windows are not big enough to work on long problems
      </td>
      <td data-label="Agent solution">Get agents to write things down</td>
      <td data-label="Human solution">Notetaking</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td data-label="Problem">Agents cannot work effectively on very big problems</td>
      <td data-label="Agent solution">
        Allow agents to invoke (networks of) subagents
      </td>
      <td data-label="Human solution">Make companies, organizations</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td data-label="Problem">
        Agents cannot do many / very complex things without being told how
      </td>
      <td data-label="Agent solution">Skills</td>
      <td data-label="Human solution">Textbooks, tutorials, trainings</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td data-label="Problem">Agents can waffle around</td>
      <td data-label="Agent solution">Leave them alone</td>
      <td data-label="Human solution">Don't micromanage</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td data-label="Problem">
        Agents don't know enough about the user to work effectively
      </td>
      <td data-label="Agent solution">Connectors</td>
      <td data-label="Human solution">Research, cross-referencing</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td data-label="Problem">
        Agents sometimes do high-impact things they shouldn't
      </td>
      <td data-label="Agent solution">
        Sandbox the agent but allow escape with approval
      </td>
      <td data-label="Human solution">Security clearance &amp; controls</td>
    </tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

I think you can reasonably predict future harness improvements by following
human knowledge tooling improvements:


<table class="agent-comparison">
  <thead>
    <tr>
      <th>Human Pattern</th>
      <th>Agent Prediction</th>
      <th>AKA</th>
    </tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr>
      <td data-label="Human Pattern">
        Hierarchies are helpful, but so is talking laterally
      </td>
      <td data-label="Prediction">Agents will talk with sibling agents</td>
      <td data-label="AKA">Direct messaging</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td data-label="Human Pattern">
        As communication increases, so does desync
      </td>
      <td data-label="Prediction">
        Agents will join highly targeted and temporary multiplayer agent
        chatrooms
      </td>
      <td data-label="AKA">Meetings</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td data-label="Human Pattern">
        People get better results when they take time away from doing work to
        think (actively or passively) about work more zoomed-out
      </td>
      <td data-label="Prediction">
        Agents will benefit from being run between goals without a stated
        problem to solve
      </td>
      <td data-label="AKA">Time off</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td data-label="Human Pattern">
        Skill creation can be selfish or prosocial, not just initiated by the
        top of the hierarchy
      </td>
      <td data-label="Prediction">
        Agents will create skills without being asked and share them with other
        agents without being asked
      </td>
      <td data-label="AKA">Innovate &amp; ask for help</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td data-label="Human Pattern">
        In large organizations people develop reputations, norms, mentorships,
        status
      </td>
      <td data-label="Prediction">
        As larger groups of agents work together, it will be helpful for them to
        develop internal judgements of other agents and sometimes share these
        with other agents selectively
      </td>
      <td data-label="AKA">Gossip</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td data-label="Human Pattern">
        Agents can be spun up and/or reused by the human, and spin-down is a
        sign of non-helpfulness
      </td>
      <td data-label="Prediction">
        Each replacement agent can slightly tweak its behavior from its
        predecessor, with some amount of randomness, to lead to better outcomes
        for the human and therefore a longer lifetime for the agent
      </td>
      <td data-label="AKA">Natural selection</td>
    </tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
